## Case 1
A 58‑year‑old hypertensive patient presents with sudden onset of hemiplegia, followed by symptoms of increased intracranial tension (headache & vomiting). A CT scan shows extensive intracerebral haemorrhage in the region of basal ganglia.

**a. What is the most likely cause of this cerebral haemorrhage?**  
Rupture of multiple microaneurysms in the lenticulostriate arteries precipitated by sudden rise in blood pressure.

**b. What are expected cause and complications in this case?**  
Rapidly expanding blood clot dissects and destroys brain tissue; it may rupture into the ventricular system or subarachnoid space; blood in the CSF causes meningeal irritation.

**c. What is the direct cause of death?**  
Tentorial herniation may cause compression of the brain stem.
