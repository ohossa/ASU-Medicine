## Section 7: Motor Questions (Short Answer)

*(Answers provided in original)*

**1. Difference between Negative stretch reflex and Inverse stretch reflex regarding stimulus and response?**
- Negative stretch reflex: Stimulus = muscle contraction (end of normal stretch reflex); Response = muscle relaxation.
- Inverse stretch reflex: Stimulus = marked stretch; Response = muscle relaxation.

**2. Importance of Jendrassik maneuver?**
- Distract the brain to decrease its inhibitory signals on anterior horn cells of the reflex.

**3. Difference in function between Putamen and Caudate circuit?**
- Putamen: planning program; Caudate: cognitive control.

**4. Explain the cause of Alpha rigidity in Parkinsonism?**
- Loss of inhibition of lentiform nucleus on vestibular nucleus (normally vestibular nucleus sends alpha impulses to AHC of antigravity muscles).

**5. Define static tremors and explain its cause?**
- Static tremors appear during rest and disappear in exercise. Cause: loss of control over the thalamus, so thalamus sends unadjusted signals to cortex causing contraction of agonist then antagonist.

**6. Mention 2 tracts controlling flexors of upper limb?**
- Lateral corticospinal and Rubrospinal tract

**7. Mention cause of dorsiflexion of big toe without fanning of other toes?**
- Lesion in area 4 only

**8. Explain one cause of hyperreflexia in chronic UMNL?**
- Denervation hypersensitivity

**9. Define Nystagmus and its cause?**
- To and fro eye movement; cause: Vestibulocerebellum lesion

**10. Difference between Servoassistant and servocomparator function?**
- Servoassistant: function of stretch reflex (load reflex); Servocomparator: function of spinocerebellum comparing intended movement from cortex with actual movement.

**11. Mention 2 signs in UMNL?**
- Clasp knife spasticity and Clonus

**12. One test to differentiate between Sensory and Motor ataxia?**
- Romberg test: positive in sensory ataxia

**13. Define Ataxia, explain cause of Trunkal ataxia?**
- Incoordination of voluntary movement in absence of muscle paralysis. Cause of trunkal ataxia: lesion in vestibulocerebellum.

**14. Mention 2 structures which discharge unadjusted signals and how they are controlled?**
- Cortex: controlled by damping function of stretch reflex; Thalamus: controlled by basal ganglia circuits (balance between direct excitatory and indirect inhibitory pathways).

**15. Function of Cerebrocerebellum?**
- Planning of next movement, not concerned with ongoing movement.

**16. Cause of pendular knee jerk?**
- Lesion in Cerebrocerebellum (leading to hypotonia).

**17. Difference between lead pipe and cog wheel rigidity?**
- (Not provided in text; typical answer: lead pipe is uniform resistance, cogwheel is ratchet-like due to tremor.)

**18. Cause of normoreflexia in Parkinsonism?**
- Alpha rigidity increases tone in agonist and antagonist equally, canceling each other during reflex.

**19. Define Motor Apraxia and its cause?**
- Loss of motor memory; cause: lesion in Area 6, SMA.

**20. Coordination between Lateral and Medial corticospinal tract?**
- Lateral controls distal limb muscles, medial controls trunk; during writing there is coordination between them.

**21. Explain Alpha Gamma coactivation?**
- During voluntary contraction, supraspinal centers activate both alpha and gamma motor neurons simultaneously.

**22. Causes of Hypertonia?**
- Lesion in Area 6, Lentiform nucleus, Paleocerebellum, Medullary RF.

**23. Cause of hypotonia in Acute UMNL?**
- Sudden withdrawal of supraspinal facilitatory impulses (gamma on AHC).

**24. Role of GPI on the thalamus?**
- Inhibitory on thalamus (indirect pathway); loss of its inhibitory effect leads to direct pathway due to inhibitory effect of caudate on GPI.

**25. Damping function in motor point of view?**
- (Refers to stretch reflex and spinocerebellum function)

**26. Structure which has intrinsic activity?**
- Pontine RF

**27. Function of SMA?**
- Complex motor plan, bimanual coordination.

**28. Site which puts the idea for voluntary movement?**
- Prefrontal association cortex

**29. Example of Disynaptic reflex and its importance?**
- Inverse stretch reflex; importance: protect muscle tendon.

**30. One polysynaptic reflex?**
- Flexor withdrawal reflex

**31. Cause of loss of superficial reflex in UMNL and LMNL?**
- UMNL: loss of control all over the reflex; LMNL: lesion of AHC or cut of efferent.

**32. Difference between Parkinsonism and Neocerebellar syndrome regarding conversation?**
- Parkinsonism: slow monotonous speech; Neocerebellar syndrome: staccato speech.

**33. Cause of Circumduction Gait in UMNL?**
- Extrapyramidal tract is intact, so area 6 sends signals to vestibulospinal tract to control proximal muscles.

**34. Physiological relation between area 4 and area 6?**
- Area 6 shares with area 4 in fine movement (30% of pyramidal tract fibers).

**35. Why patient fails to stop his finger at the nose?**
- Loss of damping (off) function of spinocerebellum.

**36. Why cerebellum is always excitatory?**
- Deep nuclear cell receives excitatory signals from climbing and mossy fibers, and inhibitory signal from Purkinje fibers.

**37. Property not present in Stretch reflex?**
- After discharge

**38. Cause of Dysdiadochokinesia?**
- Inability to do rapid alternating movement due to lesion in Cerebrocerebellum.

**39. Cause of failure to swing arm during walking; gait abnormality?**
- Basal ganglia lesion (Parkinsonism); gait: shuffling gait.

**40. Can you treat Parkinsonism by Dopamine administration?**
- No, dopamine doesn't cross BBB; give L‑Dopa which crosses and converts to dopamine.

**41. Types of Ataxia and cause of each type?**
- Sensory: Tabes dorsalis; Motor: Cerebellar disease; Trunkal: Vestibulocerebellum lesion.
