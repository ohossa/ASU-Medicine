• PHYSIOLOGY { CNS 2}
Q1. Define Motivation and mention the role of the limbic
system. (3 Marks)
• Motivation: Motivation is the internal force or drive that
activates, directs, or inhibits certain behaviors in order to
achieve a specific goal.
Role of Limbic System:
• Reward system
Located in nucleus accumbens and medial forebrain bundle.
Produces pleasure and satisfaction.
Neurotransmitter involved: Dopamine.
• Punishment system
Located in periaqueductal gray and hypothalamus.
Causes avoidance behavior and inhibition of actions.
Q2. Compare REM and Non-REM sleep. (2 Marks)
• Non-REM Sleep :
Dreams are rare, simple and fragmented.
Muscle tone is present.
Sleep walking and sleep talking occur.
Arousal threshold is low. (Eaiser to wake )

• REM Sleep :
Dreams are vivid and frequent.
Characterized by Muscle atonia.
No sleep walking.
Arousal threshold is high. ( Difficult to wake)
Q3. Enumerate types of Implicit (Non-declarative)
Memory with brain centers. (2 Marks)
• Procedural Memory
Concerned with skills and habits.
Brain centers: Striatum and Cerebellum.
• Priming
Previous exposure improves recognition or performance.
Brain center: Neocortex.
• Associative Learning
Learning by association between stimuli.
Brain centers:
Amygdala for emotional responses.
Cerebellum for motor responses.
• Non-associative Learning
Includes habituation and sensitization.
Associated Brain centers: Reflex pathways.

Q4. Compare Short-Term and Long-Term Memory. (2
Marks)
Capacity of Storage :
• Short-term memory has a limited capacity and can store
about 7-10 items.
• Long-term memory has a virtually unlimited capacity.
Duration:
• Short-term memory lasts for seconds to minutes.
• Long-term memory lasts for days, years, or a lifetime.
Remembering Time :
Recall from short-term memory is immediate.
Recall from long-term memory requires search and retrieval.
Q5. Mention neurotransmitters involved in arousal with
their origin. (1.5marks)
• Norepinephrine: Originates from the Locus
Coeruleus.
• Serotonin: Orginates from the Raphe nuclei located
within the Reticular formation.
• Histamine: Originates from the Tuberomamillary
neurons in the Posterior Hypothalamus.

Q6: Mention factors that activate and inhibit the
Reticular Activating System (RAS). (2 Marks)
• Activators: These include pain signals traveling via the
Spinoreticular tract and various proprioceptive impulses.
• Inhibitors: These include general anesthetics and the
neurotransmitter Serotonin, which is released from the
Raphe nuclei.
Q7. Mention types of Dysarthria with causes. (1.5 Marks)
• Ataxic Dysarthria: Lesions in the Cerebellum.
• Spastic Dysarthria: Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) lesions.
• Flaccid Dysarthria: Lower Motor Neuron (LMN) lesions.
Q8:Mention three functions of the prefrontal association
area. (3 Marks)
• Motor Planning: Planning of complex sequences of
motor movements.
• Working Memory: Temporary storage of information
during intellectual tasks.
• Higher Functions: Execution of personality, moral
judgment, social behavior, and decision-making.

Q9: Mention the clinical value of the
Electroencephalogram (EEG).
• Diagnosis of Epilepsy: Identifies abnormal electrical
activity and helps classify seizure types.
• Sleep Disorders: Assesses sleep stages and aids in
diagnosing conditions like narcolepsy or sleep apnea.
• Brain Death: Confirms complete absence of cerebral
electrical activity.
Q10: Mention the functions of the Limbic Association
Area.
• Behavior and Emotion: Regulates emotional responses
and social behavior.
• Motivation: Provides the internal drive to perform tasks.
• Memory Integration: Links emotions to long-term
memory storage.
Q11: Define "Positive Reinforcement" and the clinical
result of damage to Exner's Area.
• Positive Reinforcement: The process of encouraging or
establishing a behavior by offering a reward when the
behavior is performed.
• Exner's Area Damage: Leads to Agraphia, the inability to
write despite normal hand motor function.

Q12: List the functions of the Hypothalamus regarding
visceral control. (2 Marks)
• Temperature regulation: Maintains core body
temperature.
• Thirst and water balance: Controls water intake and
ADH-mediated water retention.
• Endocrine control: Regulates anterior pituitary hormone
release.
• Circadian rhythms: Controls sleep-wake cycles via the
suprachiasmatic nucleus.
Q13: Define Positive Reinforcement and explain its
physiological basis. (1.5 Marks)
• Definition: Strengthening of a behavior by presenting a
rewarding stimulus.
• Physiological basis: Mediated by the limbic reward
system, primarily through dopamine release in the
nucleus accumbens.
