PATHOLOGY { CNS 2} + MICRO
Q1: List neurodegenerative diseases according to the
specific brain regions they affect. (2 Marks)
• Cerebral Cortex: Alzheimer's disease and
Frontotemporal dementia.
• Basal Ganglia: Parkinson's disease, Huntington's
disease, and Chorea.
• Cerebellum: Motor ataxia and Spinocerebellar ataxia.
• Motor Neurons: Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS).
Q2: Describe the pathogenesis and microscopic features
of Parkinson's Disease. (2 Marks)
• Pathogenesis: It is caused by the progressive loss of
dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra.
• Microscopic Features: It is characterized by the loss of
dopaminergic neurons and gliosis.
• Key Finding: The presence of Lewy bodies, which are
intracytoplasmic eosinophilic inclusions containing
alpha-synuclein accumulation.

Q3: Mention the clinical picture (symptoms) of
Parkinson's Disease. (2 Marks)
• Tremors: Characteristic pill-rolling tremors.
• Posture and Gait: A flexion attitude (stooped posture)
and a shuffling gait.
• Other Signs: Monotone speech, generalized rigidity, and
normoreflexia.
Q4: Describe the pathogenesis and gross features of
Huntington's Disease. (2 Marks)
• Pathogenesis: It is an autosomal dominant disorder
caused by a CAG trinucleotide repeat mutation in the
HTT gene on chromosome 4p.
• Gross Features: The brain is shrunken with marked
atrophy of the caudate nucleus and putamen.
• Cortical Change: Atrophy is also commonly seen in the
frontal lobe.
Q5: Mention the microscopic features of Huntington's
Disease. (1Marks)
• Cell Loss: Severe loss of GABA-releasing neurons and
associated gliosis.
• Inclusions: The presence of intranuclear inclusions
containing ubiquitinated Huntington protein is
diagnostic.

Q6: What are the characteristics and microscopic
features of Medulloblastoma? (2 Marks)
• Characteristics: It is a highly malignant (Grade 4)
embryonal neuroepithelial tumor that is most common
in children and is notably radiosensitive.
• Microscopic Features: It presents with malignant blue
cells arranged in Homer Wright rosettes (cells
surrounding a central fibrillar space or blood vessels).
Q7: Mention the characteristics of Hemangioblastoma.
(1 Marks)
• Nature: It is a Grade 1, highly vascular tumor of
unknown cellular origin.
• Associations: It is frequently associated with Von Hippel-
Lindau (VHL) disease.
• MICROBIOLOGY (CNS 2)

Q1: Mention the main causative organisms and CSF
findings in Acute Bacterial Meningitis. (2 Marks)
Causative Organisms:
• Neisseria meningitidis,
• Streptococcus pneumoniae,
• Haemophilus influenzae type b.
CSF Appearance: Turbid or purulent.
• Markedly decreased Glucose.
• Highly Elevated Protein,
• High neutrophil count (Leukocytosis).
Q2: Explain why local anesthesia is less effective in
infected tissues. (2 Marks)
• pH Change: Infection/inflammation increases ECF
acidity (lower pH).
• Effect on Drug: Anesthetics (weak bases) become
ionized in acidic environments, preventing them from
penetrating the nerve membrane.
OR
• Repeated infections increase the acidity of the
extracellular fluid, which decreases the activity of local
anesthetics (e.g., Lidocaine HCL).

Q3: Transmission, Pathogenesis and clinical stages of
Rabies. (1 Marks)
Transmission:
• Usually transmitted through the bite of a rabid animal;
virus present in saliva.
Pathogenesis:
• Rabies virus replicates locally and travels along
peripheral nerves to the CNS, causing encephalitis.
Stages:
• Prodromal (fever), Excitative (hydrophobia), and Paralytic
(coma).
Q4: Mechanism of Tetanus and Botulism toxins. (1
Marks)
• Tetanus: Blocks inhibitory neurotransmitters (GABA/
Glycine) leading to spastic paralysis and muscle rigidity.
• Botulism: Blocks Acetylcholine release leading to flaccid
paralysis.
