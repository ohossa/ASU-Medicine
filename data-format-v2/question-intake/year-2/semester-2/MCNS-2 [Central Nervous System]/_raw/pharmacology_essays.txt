Q1: How to manage Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome
(NMS)? (1 Mark)
• Discontinue the antipsychotic.
• Administer muscle relaxants (Dantrolene or Diazepam).
• Dopamine agonists (Bromocriptine).
• Provide intensive cooling and IV hydration to prevent
renal failure.
Q2: Rationalize the addition of vasoconstrictors (e.g.,
Epinephrine) to local anesthesia.
• Prolong Duration: Decreases local blood flow, slowing
drug removal and increasing the duration of action.
• Reduce Toxicity: Slower systemic absorption prevents
high peak plasma levels, reducing toxicity risk.
• Hemostasis: Causes local vasoconstriction, which
minimizes bleeding at the procedure site.

Q3: List the drugs used to prevent Alcohol Abuse relapse
and their MOA.

• Naltrexone: An opioid receptor antagonist that reduces

• Acamprosate: Balance glutamate/GABA to decrease

combination (poly) therapy? (1Mark)
• Drug Interactions:
• To avoid complex drug-drug interactions, as
antiepileptics are often enzyme inducers or inhibitors.

Q5: Mention the clinical uses of Sodium Bicarbonate
(NaHCo_3) in CNS emergencies.
• ТСА Toxicity: Alkalinizes blood to prevent cardiac
arrhythmias by stabilizing sodium channels.
• Local Anesthesia: Increases non-ionized fraction of the
drug for faster onset and better efficacy.

Q7: Compare Typical and Atypical Antipsychotics
regarding side effects and efficacy:
(A) Side effects:
• Typical antipsychotics have a high risk of extrapyramidal
side effects (EPS).
• Atypical antipsychotics (e.g.Clozapine) have a lower risk of
EPS but are associated with metabolic side effects (weight
gain, diabetes).
(B) Efficacy:
• Typical antipsychotics are more effective for positive
symptoms of schizophrenia.
• Atypical antipsychotics are more effective for negative
symptoms (social withdrawal, apathy).
Q8: Advantages of Atypical over Typical Antipsychotics.
• Less extrapyramidal side effects and more efficacy in
treating negative symptoms.
Q9: Mention the specific pharmacotherapy for Multiple
Sclerosis walking impairment and its mechanism.
• Drug: Fampridine (Dalfampridine)
• Mechanism: Blocks potassium (K+) channels, improving
nerve conduction in demyelinated axons and enhancing
walking ability.

Q10: Mention clinical uses of antidepressants other than
for psychosis. (1 Mark)
• Nocturnal Enuresis: Managed with Imipramine (TCA).
• Urinary Incontinence: Managed with Duloxetine (SNRI).
• Neuropathic Pain: Managed with TCAs or NDRIs.
Q11: Explain the management protocol for Lithium (Li)
toxicity. (1 Mark)
• Stop Lithium administration immediately.
• Increase fluid intake.
• Perform Hemodialysis for severe cases.
Q12: Why are Valproate and Phenytoin particularly
avoided in female patients? (1 Mark)
Avoided due to there effects-
• Valproate: Associated with Polycystic Ovary Syndrome
(PCOS), hair loss (alopecia), and weight gain.
• Phenytoin: Causes cosmetic side effects like Hirsutism,
acne, and Gingival Hyperplasia (gum overgrowth).

Q13: Rationalize why Gentamicin is only used in the first
line for 7-10 days.
• Gentamicin is highly nephrotoxic and ototoxic;
prolonged use beyond 7-10 days significantly increases
the risk of irreversible kidney damage and permanent
hearing loss.
Q14: Rationalize the preference for Liposomal
Amphotericin B over ordinary Amphotericin B in Candida
Meningitis.
• Reduced Toxicity: Liposomal formulation decreases
infusion-related reactions and nephrotoxicity.
• Better CNS Penetration: Achieves higher concentrations
in the CNS with fewer systemic side effects.
Q15: List antibiotics that poorly cross the blood-brain
barrier (BBB) and must be injected intrathecally.
Injectable Intrathecally:
• Aminoglycosides (e.g., Gentamicin)
• Vancomycin when the BBB is intact.
• Contraindicated Intrathecally: Penicillins.

Q16: Explain the drug choices for Amyotrophic Lateral
Sclerosis (ALS) and Multiple Sclerosis (MS) walking
improvement.
• ALS: Riluzole - Sodium (Nat) channel blocker that slows
disease progression.
• MS: Fampridine (Dalfampridine) - Potassium (K+) channel
blocker used to improve walking speed.
Q17: Why is Sevoflurane preferred for inhalation
anesthesia induction in children?
• Sevoflurane is non-irritating, allowing for smooth, rapid
induction without causing breath-holding or coughing.
Q18: Mention the first-line therapy for Mucormycosis.
• Liposomal Amphotericin B is the treatment of choice,
often combined with surgical debridement of infected
tissue.
